- `figures/fig_sensitivity_tornado.png`: One-at-a-time sensitivity of the safety margin at the case-study corner (v = 30 m/s, r = 60 m, Table 1) to a 10 % decrease (grey) or increase (colour) of each input. The dashed line marks the feasibility limit M = 0.
- `figures/fig_monte_carlo.png`: Monte Carlo analysis, 5000 samples per circuit and panel (seed 20261122). (b) Corner demand a_ref drawn uniformly from 10 to 30 m/s^2 with the same uncertainty on the other inputs: share of samples in which each unconstrained ranking recommends a setup that fails at least one corner although a feasible setup existed, which the gated VDPM ranking recommends instead. Samples in which no setup was feasible are excluded from this count. Error bars: 95 % Wilson intervals.
- `figures/fig_demand_sweep.png`: Minimum safety margin over all corners of each synthetic reference circuit as the corner demand a_ref (v^2 / r at every corner) increases. Red: the drag-only ranking recommends a setup that fails at least one corner while a feasible setup exists, which the gated VDPM ranking recommends instead. Grey: no setup is feasible and VDPM makes no recommendation.
- `figures/fig_model_curves.png`: Curves computed from Eqs. 4.1 to 4.5 with the inputs of Table 1 (m = 800 kg, A = 1.5 m^2, rho = 1.225 kg/m^3, mu = 1.5, g = 9.81 m/s^2). (b) Beyond the critical radius r* the simplified model sets no grip-limited speed. (c) At fixed radius the margin falls with speed because demand grows with v^2 while only the aerodynamic part of the grip does.

## Scope

All results use the manuscript's simplified model (planar constant-radius corners, constant friction, no load transfer) and synthetic circuit layouts that match the published summary statistics. They demonstrate the method; they are not predictions of real lap-time performance.
